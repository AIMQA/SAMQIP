The Team Workbook (RES-03) bundles project tracking, a PDSA log and an 18-item SQUIRE check into one spreadsheet. Its PDSA Log duplicates RES-06 and its SQUIRE Check duplicates the self-check built into RES-10 — pick one home for each, not both, so you’re not filling in the same thing twice. Its Data Log is not a duplicate of the Audit Data Template (RES-08): Data Log holds your de-identified weekly counts for the run chart, while the Audit Data Template holds identifiable patient-level data and stays on NHS systems only. The Data Log’s built-in chart is a plain run chart; RES-07 is the one to use when you need the median, shift signals and control limits.

Where to start — SAM-QI’s priority first

PRIORITY TOPIC

Same Day Emergency Care

SAM’s QI committee ran a four-round Delphi to decide where acute medicine QI effort should go, and the answer was SDEC (Kennedy et al., Acute Medicine 2022). A second, multi-professional Delphi then ranked SDEC quality metrics (Subbe et al., Clinical Medicine 2024): the system measures came out on top — above all, the proportion of patients who get same-day care, in and out of SDEC units. If you work in or near SDEC, your baseline measure already has a name.

Who gets same-day care?The share of eligible patients reaching SDEC rather than a bed — and the conversion rate back to admission.
Time to decisionArrival to senior decision in SDEC, and the hours SDEC is actually open against the hours it is meant to be.
One pathway, done properlyIron-deficiency anaemia, DVT/PE, cellulitis, low-risk chest pain — pick one and measure work-up completion before discharge.
Referral inHow quickly GP, ED and 111 referrals reach SDEC — and what happens on the “no” route.
WARD STANDARDS THAT ALWAYS NEED WORK

NEWS2 escalation on the take · VTE assessment within 14 hours · the AKI bundle and time to senior review · 4AT for over-65s · Sepsis Six timing · discharge-summary turnaround and content · medicines reconciliation within 24 hours · treatment-escalation plans documented within 24 hours.

Suggested themes, not live projects — pick the one that bugs you most on your own ward, then count something. Twenty sets of notes is enough to start.

SAM support network

Where help comes from

Unit → region → SAM

TIER 01 · WEEKLY

Your unit’s QI team

A short QI huddle on the AMU or SDEC every week: a consultant lead, at least one nurse, pharmacist or therapist, and whoever is running a project. Pitch here. Register here, with your trust’s clinical audit or QI office. Bring the run chart here when there’s something to look at.

Don’t have one? The Roadmap and the Team Workbook are designed so that three people and a whiteboard can start one this month.

Come here forIdeas, the pitch, registration, supervision, the mid-project “is this working?” — and the decision to keep, tweak or drop.
TIER 02 · REGIONAL

Your regional network

Acute medicine training programmes and regional QI networks: somewhere to present once your unit has seen the chart, to find someone who has run something similar, and to see what neighbouring units are doing about the same problem. Ask your training programme director or regional QI lead who runs it where you are.

Regional and unit networks are welcome to copy this page and the kit for their own use.

Come here forA second opinion, a bigger audience, a template someone has already adapted, and the regional meeting or poster slot.
TIER 03 · NATIONAL

SAM

The kit on this page and the Learn page. The library of completed projects. The Quality Improvement category at the SAM conference — run your draft through the QI abstract checker first. And a set of programmes a project can plug straight into:

  • SAMBA — the national benchmarking audit; if your unit takes part, your baseline numbers may already exist.
  • SAM-PLER — patient-reported experience in acute medicine; the ready-made way to hear from patients.
  • SAM SitRep — rapid national snapshots of pressure on acute medical services.
  • SAM Improvement Exchange — SAM’s route for sharing QI work nationally.
  • Acute Medics as Improvers and Acute Medics Leading Improvement — SAM’s QI courses with Aqua, plus QI certification.

The SAM inbox is not a helpdesk, and that’s deliberate: local help is faster and knows your ward.

Get involved

Start local. Finish it. Then send it in.

01

Start on your unit

Find a problem that bugs you, count something — twenty sets of notes is enough — and pitch it at your unit’s next QI meeting. Register it with your trust’s audit or QI office. Your local QI lead is your first port of call, start to finish.

02

Get help nearby

Stuck, or unsure whether the data is telling you anything? Ask your unit’s QI team, then your regional network. That is where support lives — see where help comes from. The SAM inbox isn’t a helpdesk.

03

Run it with the kit

Roadmap open, Workbook shared, one run chart. Use the kit as it stands so your data and write-up come out in a shape the library can take without rework.

04

Submit when it’s finished

Completed projects only: at least one full PDSA cycle, post-change data, the 18-item SQUIRE check done. Send it to the SAM QIP team and, if it fits, it goes into the library with your name on it.

About the hub

Built for the whole multidisciplinary team

SAM members work across every part of acute care — doctors, nurses, pharmacists, therapists and the wider MDT — and a QIP that only lives with one profession tends to stall the moment that person moves on. This hub exists to keep projects owned by the whole team: a shared way of running them, a shared record of what’s been tried and what worked, and a clear answer to “who do I ask?”, whatever your role.

Four things live here. The kit — twelve pieces, from a one-page Start Here to a SQUIRE self-check, with the seven-stage Roadmap as its spine. The library — completed projects submitted by SAM members, so the next unit doesn’t start from scratch. The Learn page — the method in five minutes, a training ladder, and what ARCP expects. And the support map — because most help should come from your own unit and region, and the SAM inbox is reserved for two things: joining the SAM QIP team, and submitting a finished project.

Privacy

This page sets no cookies and runs no analytics. The typefaces load from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address when they load; nothing else leaves your browser. Files you download are yours to keep. Nothing in the kit asks for patient-identifiable data except the Audit Data Template, which is designed to stay on NHS systems.

Accessibility

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 AA: everything works with a keyboard, text can be resized to 200% without loss, colours meet contrast requirements, and the page respects your system’s dark mode and reduced-motion settings. The downloadable files are standard Office and PDF formats. If something here doesn’t work for you, email quality.improvement@acutemedicine.org.uk with “Accessibility” in the subject. The Diagram Tool has its own accessibility statement.
